Health Impacts of Air Pollution – A Public Health Perspective

Health Impacts

Air pollution is recognized as one of the leading environmental risk factors affecting global public health. Long-term and short-term exposure to polluted air has been strongly associated with increased morbidity and mortality across all age groups.

According to the World Health Organization, air pollution is responsible for millions of premature deaths each year, making it one of the most significant preventable health threats worldwide.


Pathways of Exposure

Air pollutants enter the human body primarily through inhalation. Fine particles and gaseous pollutants penetrate deep into the respiratory system, triggering inflammatory responses and oxidative stress.

  • PM2.5 can pass from lungs into the bloodstream
  • Gaseous pollutants irritate airways and damage lung tissue
  • Chronic exposure leads to cumulative biological damage

Respiratory Health Effects

1. Short-Term Effects

  • Irritation of airways
  • Coughing and wheezing
  • Shortness of breath
  • Increased asthma attacks

Children, elderly individuals, and people with pre-existing respiratory conditions are particularly vulnerable.


2 Long-Term Effects

  • Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
  • Reduced lung growth in children
  • Permanent lung function impairment
  • Increased risk of lung cancer

Fine particulate matter is classified as a carcinogenic agent by international health authorities.


Cardiovascular Impacts

Air pollution significantly increases the risk of cardiovascular diseases.

Key Health Outcomes

  • Heart attacks
  • Stroke
  • Hypertension
  • Irregular heartbeat

Studies show that prolonged exposure to PM2.5 causes inflammation of blood vessels, accelerates atherosclerosis, and increases blood clot formation.


Impact on Children and Development

Children are among the most affected populations due to:

  • Developing lungs and immune systems
  • Higher breathing rates relative to body size

Observed Effects

  • Impaired lung development
  • Increased respiratory infections
  • Cognitive development delays
  • Lower academic performance

Prenatal exposure has also been linked to low birth weight and premature births.
