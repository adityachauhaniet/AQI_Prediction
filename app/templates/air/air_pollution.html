Air Pollution

What is Air Pollution

Air pollution occurs when physical, chemical, or biological agents alter the natural composition of the atmosphere. These pollutants may be present in the form of gases, particulate matter, or aerosols and can remain suspended in the air for extended periods.

Pollutants are broadly classified into:

  • Primary pollutants – directly emitted into the atmosphere
  • Secondary pollutants – formed through chemical reactions in the atmosphere

Major Air Pollutants

1. Particulate Matter (PM)

Particulate Matter consists of microscopic solid or liquid particles suspended in the air.

  • PM10: Particles with a diameter ≤ 10 micrometers
  • PM2.5: Fine particles ≤ 2.5 micrometers

PM2.5 is considered particularly dangerous because it can penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream, causing systemic health effects.


2. Carbon Monoxide (CO)

Carbon Monoxide is a colorless, odorless gas produced by incomplete combustion of carbon-based fuels. High concentrations reduce the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood, leading to serious cardiovascular and neurological risks.


3. Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂)

Nitrogen Dioxide is mainly generated from vehicle engines and power plants. It plays a major role in the formation of secondary pollutants such as ozone and particulate matter.


4. Ozone (O₃)

Ground-level ozone is not emitted directly. It forms when nitrogen oxides react with volatile organic compounds in the presence of sunlight. While ozone in the upper atmosphere protects life, ground-level ozone is harmful to respiratory health.


5. Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)

Sulfur Dioxide is released primarily from burning coal and oil at power plants and refineries. It contributes to acid rain and fine particulate formation.


Sources of Air Pollution

1. Anthropogenic (Human-Caused) Sources

  • Road transportation
  • Industrial manufacturing
  • Thermal power generation
  • Construction and demolition activities
  • Waste burning and landfill emissions

These sources account for the majority of urban air pollution globally.


2. Natural Sources

  • Wildfires
  • Dust storms
  • Volcanic eruptions
  • Sea salt aerosols

Although natural sources contribute to air pollution, their impact is often episodic and region-specific compared to continuous anthropogenic emissions.
